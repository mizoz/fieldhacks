3. Teach it your site

Slope winds follow sunlight on the slope. Describe where the logger stood and SlopeShift learns how its switch follows the sun.

On flat ground or a shore, set the slope to 0: the switch then follows sunrise and sunset. Use a phone compass for the direction the slope faces. Ridges: compass bearing : height above level in degrees, e.g. a ridge to the east rising 8° is 90:8. The demo site is a 20° slope facing south-east near Calgary.

4. Forecast the switch

Predicted switch windows (80% of days) for any dates, from the sun's path and what this site taught it.

How it works

Each morning the analyzer takes the steady pre-dawn wind (03:00–06:00) as a baseline, slides a 20-minute average forward, and calls the first lasting change of more than 90° the switch. Directions are averaged as arrows, so north-west and north-east average to north. Evenings work the same way from a 13:00–16:00 baseline. Days without a steady baseline, or with no lasting change, are skipped rather than guessed. These are usually days when the weather overrode the slope winds.

To forecast, it compares each day's switch with the minute direct sun first reached the slope (or last left it in the evening), using the sun's position for your location, the slope and any ridges. The typical lag and its spread become your site's model, and the forecast applies it to the sun's path on any date. Read the science in the SlopeShift article.

Times use your browser's time-zone database. Alberta, for example, stays on UTC−6 year-round from 2026; make sure your device is up to date.
